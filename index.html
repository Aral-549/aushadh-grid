What the plan changes

Days without stock before routine resupply arrives, if nothing is done vs if the transfers go out today

Each medicine line is simulated day by day over its own resupply window (7 to 14 days) against its forecast. A transfer arrives the day after dispatch at the earliest; the donor gives up the stock today. Demand that cannot be met on a day is counted as lost, because patients go without rather than wait. Warehouse indents arrive with routine resupply, so they are not counted here.

    How to read this console

    Every number comes from the same pipeline: clinic visit counts are screened for outbreaks, each PHC's stock history is cleaned, demand is forecast 14 days ahead, and a transfer planner moves stock before it runs out or expires. Change the Demand scenario or District at the top and every view recalculates. Click any PHC on the map to open its forecast.

    Detection uses the CDC EARS C2 method: today's count is compared with the mean and spread of a 7-day baseline that ends 2 days earlier, so a slow rise cannot hide itself. Three standard deviations above baseline on 2 of the last 3 days raises an alert. An alert can switch the demand forecast into outbreak mode for exactly the districts affected, days before an outbreak is officially declared.

    Medicine stock and demand forecast

    Forecast: Holt linear smoothing on day-of-week adjusted consumption (120 days). Before fitting, days when the PHC had run out are replaced with estimated true demand (a stock-out records zero use, which would teach the model that demand is low), and data-entry spikes are capped. Where a PHC's report is overdue, today's stock is estimated from forecast use since the last report and the uncertainty is widened.

    Projected loss by medicine

    ₹ value, and how much transfers recover

    A lot that a quiet PHC cannot use before its expiry date is sent to a busier PHC that will use it in time. That PHC then draws on this stock first, and its own later-expiring stock lasts longer.

    Recommended cross-PHC transfers

    Two kinds of transfer. Prevent stock-out moves surplus to PHCs that would run out before resupply. Vital medicines are served first, nearest donor first, and donors with expiring stock are preferred. Rescue expiring stock moves lots that would expire unused to PHCs that will use them in time. Cold-chain items (2–8 °C) move only between PHCs with a working ice-lined refrigerator, within 150 km.

    Vehicle runs

    Each district vehicle leaves its headquarters, collects from donor PHCs, delivers to receivers and returns. A transfer joins a run only if that costs no more road km than a separate trip, and a run stays within an 8-hour day (35 km/h plus 15 minutes a stop). Runs carrying vaccines, insulin or oxytocin need a cold box.

    Shared demand model across BRICS health systems

    Each national node trains the demand model on its own PHC data. Only model weights leave the country; patient and facility records stay local. The coordinator averages weights by sample count (FedAvg) and sends the global model back. Nodes with little data, and little outbreak history, gain the most.
